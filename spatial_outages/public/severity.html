How device downtime becomes an attributed outage

A device going down is raw evidence. The engine first exits CSP-wide failures, then groups the remaining spatially connected devices into fluid polygons for local attribution.

Two decision levelsCSP × time window first. Fluid polygon for everything that remains.

Problem

Individual devices fail for many unrelated reasons. A CSP-wide failure should not create hundreds of false local polygons, while static cells create artificial borders for the failures that remain.

Definition of an outage polygon

After CSP-wide exits are removed, nearby failed devices form outage groups. When two polygons are compared in time, their outage intervals must intersect for at least 15 minutes.

Eligible devices ≥ 10Distinct remaining active devices whose coordinates fall inside the candidate geometry.
AND
Affected share ≥ 75%Affected share is distinct affected devices divided by eligible devices. The boundary is inclusive.
10 is the polygon minimum9 eligible devices can never produce an outage polygon.
75% is inclusive8 of 10 is 80% and qualifies; 7 of 10 does not.
Polygon time overlap is 15 minutesUse the intersection of the two polygon outage intervals. Time outside that intersection is not capped.

Build one top-down evidence path

Raw devices feed the CSP-wide exit first; only the remaining devices feed fluid polygons.

Technical architecture

Sources
Active customer devicesDevice ID, active status, and address key
Coordinates and CSPLatitude, longitude, and CSP ID
Outage membershipDevice ID, source outage ID, and failure and recovery times
Outputs
CSP-wide exitCSP ID, time window, eligible and down devices, affected share

One source flow: active customer devices link to their location, CSP, and outage membership through the device and address keys. CSP-wide exits keep their CSP-window key; polygons receive a derived ID. Both retain every source outage_id.

2

Run the device-first outage algorithm

Eight ordered steps turn device downtime into stable outage events.

Outage detection algorithm

Run these steps in order for every evaluation window. Devices form the groups; polygons never decide which failures belong together.

1

Measure each provider

Calculate the share of active devices currently down.

down_rate = down / active
2

Remove wide failures

Publish the provider-wide outage and exclude its devices from local detection.

down_rate >= 80%
3

Connect nearby failures

Connect failed devices that are close enough and have overlapping downtime.

distance <= MAX_DISTANCE
device time overlap > 0
4

Form outage groups

Every connected set of failed devices becomes one local outage candidate.

connected group = candidate
5

Draw the polygons

Draw one map outline around each connected device group.

group → polygon
6

Count each device once

Failed devices keep their group. A shared working device goes to the nearest failed device.

distance tie → lower polygon ID
7

Qualify the outage

Reject groups that fail either minimum.

active >= 10
affected_rate >= 75%
8

Track it across time

Polygon versions keep one event ID when the time, member, and area checks pass.

time >= 15 min
members >= 50%
area >= 20%

15-minute polygon-time overlap

Compare the outage interval of polygon A with the outage interval of polygon B.

Overlap startlater polygon start
Overlap endearlier polygon end
Overlap durationoverlap end - overlap start
Pass: A = 4:15–6:00, B = 5:45–7:00 → overlap = 5:45–6:00 = 15 minutes
Fail: A = 4:15–6:00, B = 5:46–7:00 → overlap = 14 minutes

No exclusive-time limit: time before or after the intersection does not reduce a valid 15-minute overlap.

Core rule: devices determine outage groups. Polygons only describe them. Polygon overlap never merges outages.

When two outage areas overlap

Main rule: devices determine outage groups. Polygons only describe those groups. Polygon overlap never merges outages.

What we findDecisionWhat happens next
Two polygons overlap in the same time window.Keep the device groups (PARTITIONED)Failed devices stay with their connected group. A working device inside both polygons goes to the group with the nearest failed device. If distance ties, the lower polygon ID wins.
Two polygon outage intervals overlap for at least 15 minutes, with at least 50% failed-device overlap and at least 20% area overlap.Continue the event (TIME_STITCHED)Keep both polygon versions and give them the same event_id. Treat 50% and 20% as starting values to back-test.

No shape-only merge: spatial overlap alone never joins polygons. The time comparison uses the actual intersection of their outage intervals.

Apply the CSP-wide rule before polygon rules

The top-down contract prevents a CSP-wide event from fragmenting into misleading local polygons.

Ordered outage attribution contract

OrderIfThenEvidence retained
0At least 80% of one CSP's complete active-device baseline is concurrently down at the evaluation time.ISP / OLTCSP ID, evaluation time, eligible and down device IDs, share, and source outages. Exclude the CSP-window before local grouping.
1A remaining candidate has fewer than 10 eligible devices, or its affected share is below 75%.NOISEEligible count, affected count, affected share, and failed gate.
2Two or more comparable CSPs are affected together inside one remaining polygon.POWER / SHARED AREACSP-level device counts, shares, and overlapping intervals inside the polygon.
3The same multi-CSP pattern appears across multiple separated outage polygons in one event.REGIONALPolygon IDs, geometries, compared CSPs, and event timing.
4One CSP is affected inside the polygon and at least one other eligible CSP is observed healthy there.FIBRE / LOCAL CSPAffected CSP evidence and healthy same-polygon comparison CSP evidence.
5The polygon qualifies but the required cross-CSP comparison is missing or conflicting.UNKNOWNAvailable evidence and the exact missing comparison.

Safety boundary: `FIBRE`, `POWER`, and `ISP / OLT` are inferred failure domains. Confirm the physical cause with OLT alarms, power telemetry, tickets, or field evidence.

How to read the dry runs

  • First: a CSP at or above 80% down exits before local device grouping.
  • Then: remaining polygons need 10 devices and 75% affected.
  • Compare: CSP Alpha is evaluated beside CSP Beta.
