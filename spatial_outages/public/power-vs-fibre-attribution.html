The proposed method

Find the real sub-outage. Then decide power or fibre.

A parent polygon can contain separate device groups. Split it with variable-density clustering, inspect the R70–R100 radius profile, then compare provider behaviour inside each group.

Observed in the portal snapshot 779

UNKNOWN outages had no peer-provider comparison

Their median R90 is 557 m; 76.3% are within 1 km. This supports testing sub-outages, not guessing a cause.

Follow one outage from polygon to verdict.

Each stage answers one question. A later stage cannot repair weak evidence from an earlier one.

2CompareWhich providers failed inside the group?

Step 1 · Split the parent outage

One long polygon may contain several real outages.

Step 4 · Classify the likely cause

Cluster first. Cause second.

Inside R90, count every eligible device: the ones that failed and the ones still working. Then calculate a failure share for each provider.

3 · Verdict

Say what the evidence supports

2+ providers each ≥70% down → likely premise power
Affected provider ≥70% down; every peer <20% down → likely fibre/network
No peer or mixed pattern → cause UNKNOWN; still report the sub-outage

The 70% and 20% values are proposed operating gates, not calibrated cutoffs. The provider-location state needed for this comparison is available for only 3,021 of 6,152 portal outages.

7 devices

does not automatically mean fibre.

A fibre cut can affect seven devices—or thousands.

The count tells us the size, not the cause.

Seven scattered failures may be unrelated.

If there is no clear provider pattern, say “we cannot tell.”

Seven failures across providers may still be power.

Always look at who failed and when.

Step 5 · Grade the evidence

Do not publish a numeric confidence yet.

The portal can show whether a local sub-outage is plausible and whether the provider pattern looks like power or fibre. It cannot yet estimate the probability that either cause is correct.

Step 6 · Confirm the cause

Say “likely” until another signal proves the cause.

VDBSCAN can support that a local sub-outage exists. Ping absence alone cannot prove why it happened.

Current confirmed training labels: 0 power · 0 fibreConfirmation closes the incident and teaches the future model.

Confirm a power cut

Use a local power alert, a power sensor, or matching failure and recovery across several providers.

power alertlocal power sensorproviders recover together

Confirm a fibre cut

Show that the devices use the same fibre branch, receive a fibre-loss alarm, or get field confirmation.

same fibre branchfibre-loss alarmfield confirmation
Store four fields for every confirmed incident. outage_id · confirmed_cause · confirmation_source · confirmed_at

After enough linked labels exist, fit a simple regularized model on earlier incidents and test it on later ones. Publish a probability only when calibration, Brier score, precision and recall beat the current rules.

The clustering and provider thresholds shown here are proposed operating gates. No numeric confidence or physical-cause probability is claimed until confirmed power and fibre incidents are linked and used for calibration.
